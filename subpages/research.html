Research

My doctoral research has two strands. The first continues VanderWerf's program of lifting the Krohn–Rhodes decomposition theory of finite semigroups to general finite algebras. The second applies these ideas, together with tame congruence theory, to decide membership in logically defined classes of regular tree languages.

The algebraic approach to formal languages begins with a theorem of Schützenberger: a regular string language is first-order definable if and only if its syntactic monoid contains no nontrivial group, that is, the monoid is aperiodic. The condition can be checked on the monoid, so first-order definability is decidable. For languages of finite trees the syntactic monoid is replaced by a finite algebra, and the corresponding question has been open for decades. Bojańczyk and Michalewski recast several problems of this kind as membership problems for classes of finite algebras built from the two-element semilattice S, in the framework of VanderWerf's wreath product decomposition of finite algebras. These are problems about finite algebras, and tame congruence theory is a natural tool for studying them.

Results

Path algebras
Every finite simple algebra of type 5 whose trace order has a least element embeds in a matrix power of S, so it recognizes only Boolean combinations of deterministic top-down tree languages. This is the counterpart, for the semilattice, of Hobby and McKenzie's representation of simple algebras of type 1 over the two-element set.
Bojańczyk's conjecture
A simulation class is a class of finite algebras closed under division, matrix powers and wreath products. Bojańczyk conjectured that the simulation class generated by S consists of exactly the algebras with no confusion in any matrix power. I proved this for three-element algebras and showed that membership is decidable in that case. The proof replaces the existing trace algebra construction, which I showed cannot suffice, with a more flexible notion of cover.
Aperiodicity
A finite algebra is aperiodic if no nontrivial group is a subreduct of any of its matrix powers. For three-element simple algebras of type 5 I gave a decidable criterion: such an algebra fails to be aperiodic exactly when the two-element group already appears in its matrix square.

Formalization in Lean

With Deirdre Haskell, Mathias Stout, Aaron Crighton and Jonas van der Schaaf, I am formalizing the model theory of valued fields in Lean 4. Mathlib's model theory is one-sorted, while valued fields are studied in languages with separate sorts for the field, the residue field and the value group. We therefore built a library for many-sorted first-order logic: syntax and semantics, Łoś's theorem, compactness, the Löwenheim–Skolem theorems, and embedding tests for relative quantifier elimination. On top of it we proved quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields, for divisible ordered abelian groups, and for algebraically closed valued fields in a three-sorted language. The proof for valued fields was written with substantial AI assistance and is checked in full by Lean. We are preparing the foundations for inclusion in Mathlib.

Separately, I am developing a Lean library for universal algebra, which Mathlib largely lacks. It currently contains clones, congruences and the first isomorphism theorem, and a complete proof of Pálfy's theorem: a finite minimal algebra with at least three elements and an essentially binary polynomial is polynomially equivalent to a vector space. This is the first structural theorem of tame congruence theory.
